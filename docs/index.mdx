# Sauna-vs-gym-cost - when a home sauna beats paying per visit

A terminal calculator for the purchase most people price by sticker and
judge by vibe: a home sauna or cold plunge turned into a dollars-per-
session number. It amortizes the gear over the sessions you actually
take, adds the electricity line most buyers skip, and prints the visit
count where the gear finally beats paying per visit elsewhere.

Python 3.8+, standard library only. No install, no network, nothing
leaves your machine.

## The method

- **Utility cost** = kilowatt-hours per session x your electric rate.
  A 2 kWh sauna sit at 17 cents is 34 cents; the tool keeps the units
  honest so a spec-sheet kWh number and a bill-grade rate meet correctly.
- **Annual sessions** = per-week habit x 52. The honest denominator,
  because amortization lives or dies on how often the thing gets used.
- **Session cost** = gear price / total sessions + utility line. Ten
  sessions into a $2,500 unit is still $251 a sit; session 1,000 is
  under four dollars.
- **Break-even** = gear price / (per-visit alternative - your utility
  line). When the alternative costs less than the electricity alone,
  the gap is negative and usage never closes it, so the tool says so
  instead of printing a number that looks like a win.

## Usage

```
$ python3 saunacost.py --gear 2500 --week 4 --kwh 2.0 --rate 0.17 \
    --alt 22 --months 36
Sessions/week                        4
Annual sessions                    208
Sessions in window                 626
Utility/session                 $0.34
Amortized cost/session          $4.34
Break-even vs $22/visit         115 visits
```

Compare two usage habits head to head:

```
$ python3 saunacost.py --compare 2 5
at 2 sessions/week  -> $8.33/session at month 36
at 5 sessions/week  -> $3.54/session at month 36
5 sessions/week wins, saves $4.79/session
```

`--json` for machine output.

## Honest limits

The tool treats the electric rate as flat, ignores install and
electrical-work costs, and assumes the per-visit alternative is a real
option you would otherwise pay for. The cost-per-session habit of
checking a gear purchase against your own usage log is where this fits;
the amortized number is only as honest as the sessions counter.
